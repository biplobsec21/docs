Purpose and reading guide

This is a table-level view of the TukTakKaj service marketplace: a Seeker posts work, TukTakers bid, payment is held in escrow, the task is completed, and the money is settled and paid out. Each stage names the tables that store its durable records.

Solid flow below describes the normal task lifecycle. Side paths describe disputes, refunds, notifications, and operational support. Table names use schema.table notation.

ref / catalog — reference and task definitions iam — users and profiles task — task lifecycle payment / finance — money movement trust / comms / billing — service and records

Primary data flow

1

Accounts and eligibility

Each person has one identity; role-specific profile records store their Seeker, Business, or TukTaker details. KYC and uploaded documents support verification.

Task definition and draft

The Seeker chooses a service category, enters its configured form answers, sets the budget and service area, and can attach files. Delivery categories can add pickup, drop-off, and stop locations.

3

Publish and discover

Publishing validates the category, required form fields, and any required locations. The task changes to open; eligible TukTakers can find it by category, area, or proximity.

4

Bids, negotiation, and selection

TukTakers submit bids. Each counter-offer round is retained separately; the Seeker accepts one bid, while other bids can be rejected, withdrawn, or expire.

5

Assignment and Seeker payment

Acceptance creates an assignment attempt with the agreed amount. A payment is initiated through a configured provider; provider callbacks are recorded and processed idempotently. A successful payment links to a finance ledger transaction.

6

Escrow hold and task execution

Payment is held against the assignment. Arrival, start, completion request, and acceptance are captured as assignment events and state changes. Status history keeps the task's status transitions.

payment.escrow_holdspayment.escrow_eventstask.assignment_eventstask.assignmentstask.task_status_history
7

Settlement and accounting

After acceptance (or the configured release path), the settlement records the gross amount and snapshots the commission, VAT, and pricing-rule IDs used. A balanced ledger transaction records the money split, and statement rows make the results readable to each user.

8

TukTaker payout and close-out

The TukTaker requests redemption from their wallet to a verified payout destination. The payout request is tied to ledger transactions; completed services can then receive ratings and feedback.

Related and exception flows

Dispute, freeze, and resolution

An open dispute may freeze escrow. Staff review messages and evidence, then resolve through a release/refund/adjustment path.

trust.disputestrust.dispute_messagestrust.dispute_evidencepayment.escrow_holdspayment.refundsfinance.manual_adjustments

Refunds and cancellations

Refunds, cancellation fees, penalties, and credits are distinct records; money changes are posted to the ledger rather than editing prior entries.

payment.refundstask.cancellationsfinance.penaltiesfinance.creditsfinance.ledger_transactions

Messages and notifications

Task conversations and user notifications are linked to their task/user context. Delivery status and notification preferences are stored separately from task state.

comms.conversationscomms.messagescomms.user_notificationscomms.notification_preferences

Integration and governance

Outbox events support reliable integration delivery; idempotency keys protect retryable requests. Audit, privacy retention, and legal holds govern sensitive data lifecycle.

Important ledger design: ledger entries are append-only and are never edited or deleted. Correct a posted movement with a reversal transaction. The ledger transaction’s task/assignment references are intentionally loose references (not foreign keys); payment, escrow, and settlement rows provide the explicit business links.

Data handling principles represented in this flow

Snapshot rules: settlements retain rule IDs and computed values so later rule changes do not rewrite history.

Money: store BDT amounts as fixed-precision numeric values; every ledger movement must balance.

Lifecycle: task and assignment status records keep workflow state and event history.

Privacy: sensitive identity documents are file metadata in ops.files; retention jobs must respect legal holds.
